Now, one thing to keep in mind when you're cooking your steaks is that if you have something like Wagyu or something that has a lot of fat, you're going to have more carryover cooking. And the reason behind it is pretty simple, but it has to do with something called specific heat capacity. The specific heat capacity of water, which is the primary component of muscle tissue, is much higher than the specific heat capacity of the fat.

Especially in something like Wagyu, you have lots of fat, which means that it's going to take less energy to raise that fat by one degree than it does for the same mass of water. Let's boil it all down — no pun intended here. If you have something like this Wagyu Denver steak that has lots of fat, you want to pull it off the heat a little sooner than you would if you had like a very lean fillet that's a choice fillet or something like that.

So keep that in mind with your cooking. It's something you might not think about, but it's easy to mess something up because you're thinking, oh, this is how I always do it. But if you have a different cut of meat with different fat content, you can get different results. And we're always after consistent results with what we know. There's a lot of fun and experimenting, but when you want like a go-to, like I guarantee, I know for a fact, this is going to work exactly the way I want — okay, you got to take those things into consideration. So one steak could cook differently from another just because of the fat content, even if they're the same weight or the same shape, etc.
